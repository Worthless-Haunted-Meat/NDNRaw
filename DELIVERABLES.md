# Deliverables

The concrete artifacts this idea produces, ordered by what unblocks the first
meetup. Rule of thumb throughout: **build the simplest version that one cell
needs, and only scale it when a second cell exists.**

## Phase 1 — Unblock Meetup #1

### 1. The Cell Starter Kit *(build first)*

A short document any parent can pick up and run a first meetup with. Contents:

- The pitch: the founding post, cleaned up — the bandwidth reframe, the
  paradox, the two-room promise. This is the recruiting tool.
- The two-room agenda: what the parents' room covers in hour one; how the
  young adults' room is set up (games available, zero pressure to use them).
- The threshold conversation: how to talk about the neurodivergence threshold
  honestly with an interested family, and how special cases get mentors with
  shared lived experience (in person or Zoom) instead of a "no."
- House rules: no diagnosis talk in the kids' room, no parents in the kids'
  room, what happens in the room stays in the room.

### 2. The Threshold Definition

One page, written plainly. The core group is for young adults who — like the
founding families' kids — can engage one-on-one but freeze in social crowds. Below and
above that band, the answer isn't exclusion; it's the mentor bench (see #6).
This document exists so the first hard conversation doesn't have to be
improvised.

### 3. The Recruiting Post

The founding Facebook post, generalized so a parent in any city can post it to
their local group and get the same response Ricky got. The original produced
"I'm in" comments within hours; that's a proven instrument. Keep the voice —
the polish should not sand off the parts that made parents feel understood.

## Phase 2 — Once One Cell Is Meeting

### 4. The Strength Catalog (v1: a shared spreadsheet)

One row per young adult: interests, hyperfocuses, observed strengths,
demonstrated skills, what they made or did at meetups. Maintained by the
parents' room, updated after each meetup.

- v1 is a spreadsheet. Not an app. An app is a Phase 4 problem.
- **Consent first:** the young adult and family approve their row and approve
  any sharing beyond the cell. This rule is set now, before there is anything
  to share, because the catalog only works if the kids trust it.

### 5. The Gig Ledger

A record of every parent-seeded gig: who was bound together, what the gig was,
what was paid, what happened. This is simultaneously the network's résumé
factory and the proof — the data that shows the model works when it's time to
tell the story to a second city (or a funder).

### 6. The Mentor Bench

A list of neurodivergent adults further down the road — including those with
additional disabilities — willing to join by Zoom or in person to guide special
cases. Commenter D (50 years in the field, "it's what I do") is the first
call for building this list.

## Phase 3 — Federation (10–20 cells in one city)

### 7. The City Directory

Which cells exist, where, who anchors each. The minimal infrastructure for
cross-cell binding — five musicians in three neighborhoods can't find each
other without it.

### 8. The Binding Playbook

How a cross-cell ensemble actually happens: who spots the match in the
catalogs, how the young adults are introduced (at their bandwidth — not thrown
into a room together), how the first parent-seeded gig is structured and paid,
who mentors the ensemble.

## Phase 4 — Beyond One City

### 9. The City-in-a-Box

Everything above, packaged: starter kit, threshold doc, recruiting post,
catalog template, ledger template, binding playbook. What a stranger in
another city needs to go from one Facebook post to a federated network.

### 10. The Platform (only if the spreadsheets break)

A real strength catalog and directory with consent controls built in. Not
before Phase 3 proves the manual version. The failure mode to avoid: building
software for a community that doesn't exist yet.

## What We Deliberately Do Not Build

- **A curriculum.** The moment there's a syllabus, it's a program, and the
  kids will know.
- **A nonprofit, yet.** Legal structure follows scale; it doesn't precede it.
  Revisit when money beyond gig payments moves through the network.
- **Anything requiring the young adults to sign up for something.** They show
  up to a room where people get them. That's the entire onboarding.
