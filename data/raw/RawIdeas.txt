So Moms and Dads: I have a 19-year-old on the spectrum.   He has done two years of college already and graduated high school.  He does have high-functioning autism.  If you talked to him directly, you wouldn't know he was on the spectrum.  You place him in a social situation and there is a noted paralysis.   I'm very proud of him, when I ask him to do things such as mow the lawn every tuesday and clean the garage, The tasks are performed satisfaction, but I  I'm realistic about the challenges he is facing.   I believe that sometimes the neurodivergence is used as a crutch to prevent from doing jobs that are customer focused like McDonald's or Chipotle.  As he grows into adulthood, when I ask him to search for a job, there is a submission and a response, but there is a drive and a fire that's lacking in following through something that you feel that you need.   Of course we can throw them out in the street, We're nowhere near that yet.  You're not gonna add the labels that people add, such as lazy or apathetic, Because if dads do that, then they are not really motivating or helping.  I do feel a 16-hour session of computer time may be way too excessive. (thats a joke, But I guarantee there's — the people that I'm trying to target will understand.)
I've met an old school friend of mine that is a life friend. I had the honor of being the best man at his wedding.  His kid is of the same level of neurodivergence as mine.  Everything I am saying here he feels.   It made me realize that while they love us, we cannot speak their language.  They do not understand our style of motivation or drive.   the very factors of success is predicated on networking.  No matter the buisiness,  networking is required.   Social events such as bars, markets,  bazzars, trade shows, concerts, competitions, comicon are all events that require someone to interact with people.  But when I try to motivate him to go out, he instantly shuts off.  I was frustrated, I felt that he didnt care and I am going to have my boy be happy with living off the teet of his family for the rest of his life (which is really every dads worst case scenario).  But I have discarded that, it isn't about me.   It is about a talented young man that literally is overwhelmed by the momentum of the world around him.   He is overwhelmed because his perception of detail is much greater than ours.  Like a denial of service attack.  We all have a baud rate threshold of acceptance,  our stimulus acceptance receptors are 2400.  Thiers have a resoultion of detail that renders whetever we teach them to filter useless, 24K.   We do not stand a chance in providing them experiences to deal with that kind of bandwith.   As wordy as this is,  THIS is the point.  They do have resources that can.  Other young men and women that age.  they can gather weekly, parents in a different room,  kids in a different room.  We aggree and a agenda.   Kids do whatever (or we could make them play games).  And dads all talk about anything that can prevent our kids from forever sucking the hairy daddy tit.  It will be organtic,  a function of the people in it.   We must define the threshold of neurodivergance.  Some may have wheelchair bound. THAT is a special case.  for that we must invoke wheelchair-autists that can be brought in or zoomed to provide guidence. 
I am going to this anyway.... but this is a calll to dads that feel me.  This is what we have to do.   As our kids interact, they have talents that gel.  The dads will interact, because we are incredibly curious of what superpower that have,  we want to see them express ir,  forage it, maybe even weapoinze it 🙂
As our sons do what they love, they are networking,   And we can help.  So...   who is in?
Ricky Vega

[Founder], I'm not a dad, obviously, but I've worked in this arena for almost 50 years. First, let me say that getting together with others who are going through the same thing (with their adult children) will be good for everyone involved.
Secondly, jobs are easier for people on the spectrum when they include doing something they love. Not only do they thrive, but they are now sought after in the job market as employers understand more an more about autism and dyslexia. It's actually a "category" of applicants that employers seek. Find something he LOVES doing and then introduce him into ways what he loves doing can support him. There are VERY successful game designers, accountants, chefs, bakers, etc.
Additionally, you can reach out to JAN (the Job Accommodation Network) who understands people with autism and helps place them in perfect settings for them, whether it's working from home or at a facility.
Let me know how else I can help. It's what I do.
